### High

#### H-001 — The explicit one-work-owner-to-many-Issues lifecycle is unrepresentable

**Locations:** `SDP-vnext-pilot/WorkflowContract.md`, lines 18, 143, 163-170,
and 263-265; `SDP-vnext-pilot/WorkDomains.md`, lines 118-124; and
`SDP-vnext-pilot/validate_pilot.py`, lines 2044-2046.

The accepted Issue #5 study and the provisional contract both say a Feature or
Refactor may have one or more Issue assignments over time. The explicit
cardinality table gives a work owner `0..N` Issues and delivery `1..N`; the
reopen/extension section directs a delivered owner to gain a new Issue and new
Slice while preserving earlier acceptance.

The inventory correctly describes `authorityIssue` as the Issue that allocated
the stable prospective ID. That allocation authority is immutable and unique.
The contract then contradicts the multi-Issue rule by saying every assignment
Issue must equal that allocating inventory authority, and the validator
enforces the contradiction with `PRIMARY_WORK_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH`.

A fresh control started from the valid terminal-Feature positive, added Issue
12 as a second Feature authority, added a second prospective Slice allocated
by Issue 12, accepted both Slices and both assignments at their own exact
candidates, used disjoint paths, made Issue 12 depend on accepted Issue 10,
completed the merge order/convergence, and rebound the canonical reservation.
The only diagnostic was `PRIMARY_WORK_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH` for the second
assignment's reference to the already-created Feature.

There is no valid alternative representation: duplicating the Feature's
inventory member violates ID uniqueness, changing its allocating Issue breaks
the first assignment, and making the second Issue primary to another record
violates the Slice-to-owner/assignment cardinality. This blocks the contract's
stated multi-Issue Feature/Refactor evolution and is central to Issue #7's
Issue/Feature/Slice cardinality challenge.

**Required rework:** distinguish immutable ID-allocation authority from the
set of later assignment authorities. Keep cross-Issue reclaim protection for
new reservations, but allow an assignment to reference an already-issued work
owner when its canonical Issue appears exactly once in that owner's
`issueAuthorities`, its new Slice IDs are allocated/reserved by that Issue,
and all ordinary owner/Slice/assignment/reservation rules agree. Repair the
contradictory sentence, add positive active and delivered multi-Issue controls,
and retain a negative for an Issue absent from the work owner's authority set.

### Medium

#### M-001 — A delivered sliced Fix need not identify accepted behavior it corrects

**Locations:** the accepted
`SDP-usage-analysis/ProposedSDPWorkflow.md`, lines 142-147;
`SDP-vnext-pilot/WorkflowContract.md`, lines 62-72 and 176;
`SDP-vnext-pilot/templates/fix.template.json`, line 15; and
`SDP-vnext-pilot/validate_pilot.py`, lines 1437-1444 and 1631-1665.

The accepted study defines a Fix as a correction to accepted behavior and
includes the affected release/work owner in its minimum record. The pilot says
the same and normalizes `affectedWork` to semantic `corrects` edges. Validation
requires an evidence-qualified affected target only inside the special
zero-Slice Fix branch.

Starting from the valid `terminal-sliced-fix-closure` control and changing only
the Fix's `affectedWork` to `[]` returns no diagnostics. The Fix remains
delivered with qualified evidence, an accepted Slice, an accepted assignment,
and no semantic link to any accepted behavior. It can therefore classify
unanchored capability or structural work as a genuine Fix despite the
owner-directed taxonomy.

**Required rework:** require every delivered/released Fix, sliced or
standalone, to have at least one qualified locally resolved `affectedWork`
target whose state/evidence establishes accepted behavior. Retain the stricter
zero-Slice risk/standalone/assignment rules, add sliced-Fix empty/proposed/
unaccepted-target negatives, and add a valid sliced-Fix affected-work control.
